Consoles

Comparing and contrasting between available consoles on the market.


Playstation 4

Pros

The Playstation 4* is a respectable console, at a price of around 300 dollars, it offers a very large game library, as well as a decent performance for games, at a consistent 30. New games for the playstation from big developers usually cross-release their games for both generations, letting you play on either console. Due to the rise of the PS5 as well as the PS4 being a dated console, video games, and accessories for the PS4 are usually at a discounted price. No compatability with Bluetooth.

Cons

The PS4's online multiplayer, and online data saving features in almost all games (Although most free-to-play games don't need PS Plus) is restricted to Playstation Plus - costing 80 dollars a year JUST to get the essential, bare-bones version. A consistent performance of ~30 fps is below the curve of modern standards of performance of modern gaming consoles. Compatability with previous consoles (PS3, PS2, PS1) is entirely reliant on virtual emulation through PS Plus, on a seperate tier called PS Plus Premium, which costs 160 dollars per year. Visual settings similar to the PC to get performance are not available. (Stuff like anti-aliasing, v-sync, frame rate, texture quality.) No compatability for Bluetooth. Game library is limited to the Playstation Store without homebrew.

*The PS4 Pro is not listed on here because it's not being sold officially anymore.

Playstation 5

Pros

The Playstation 5 was a new console released by Playstation in 2020. At an MSRP of $500, it offers a great performance enhancement over the previous Playstation 4, at a consistent FPS of 60. Visual settings similar to the PC to get performance are available. (Stuff like anti-aliasing, v-sync, frame rate, texture quality.) It has built-in backwards compatability with the PS4, as long as a PS4 controller is connected to play the PS4 games.

Cons

The PS5's online multiplayer, and online data saving features in almost all games (Although most free-to-play games don't need PS Plus) is restricted to Playstation Plus - costing 80 dollars a year JUST to get the essential, bare-bones version. Compatability with previous consoles except the PS4 (PS3, PS2, PS1) is entirely reliant on virtual emulation through PS Plus, on a seperate tier called PS Plus Premium, which costs 160 dollars per year. No compatability for Bluetooth. Game library is limited to the Playstation Store without homebrew.

*The digital edition is not listed on here for the sake of concistency.

Xbox One

Pros

The Xbox One was a console released by Microsoft in 2013. It's a respectable console, at a price of around 250 dollars, it offers a large game library, as well as a decent performance for games, at a consistent 30. New games for the Xbox One, developers usually cross-release their games for both generations, letting you play on either consoles. It has built-in backwards compatability with the Xbox 360 and the Xbox, allowing for an extensive library of games to be played.

Cons

A consistent performance of ~30 fps is below the curve of modern standards of performance of modern gaming consoles. The Xbox One's online multiplayer is restricted to Xbox Game Pass - costing 60 dollars a year JUST to get the essential, bare-bones version. Visual settings similar to the PC to get performance are not available. (Stuff like anti-aliasing, v-sync, frame rate, texture quality.) No compatability for Bluetooth. Game library is limited to the Microsoft Store without homebrew.

Xbox Series X/S

Pros

The Xbox Series X and S series were a new console series released by Microsoft in 2020. At an MSRP of $500/$300, both offer a great performance enhancement over the previous Xbox One, at a consistent FPS of around 120. Visual settings similar to the PC to get performance are available in some games. (Stuff like anti-aliasing, v-sync, frame rate, texture quality.) It has built-in backwards compatability with the Xbox One, Xbox 360, and original Xbox.

Cons

The Xbox Series X/S's online multiplayer is restricted to Xbox Game Pass - costing 60 dollars a year JUST to get the essential, bare-bones version. No compatability for Bluetooth. Game library is limited to the Microsoft Store without homebrew.

Nintendo Switch

Pros

The Switch* was a console released in 2017 by Nintendo. At a price at around $250, it is very inexpensive compared to competitors. It offers a very large game library due to it's age. It has a decent performance for games, at a consistent 30 FPS. Has compatability with Bluetooth devices.

Cons

The Switch's online multiplayer, and online data saving features in almost all games (Although most free-to-play games don't need Switch Online) is restricted to Switch Online - costing 20 dollars a year. A consistent performance of ~30 fps is below the curve of modern standards of performance of modern gaming consoles. Compatability with previous consoles is entirely reliant on virtual emulation through Nintendo Switch Online, which costs 20 dollars per year (for individuals). Visual settings similar to the PC to get performance are not available. (Stuff like anti-aliasing, v-sync, frame rate, texture quality.) Game library is limited to the Nintendo Store without homebrew.

*The Switch OLED and Switch Lite are not mentioned for consistency.

PC

Pros

The personal computer has a wide range of games available for it. ~70,000 are on the biggest platform, Steam, but overall, the estimate of all available PC games is around 5,000,000. Needless to say, this offers a wide range of games able to be played on the platform. Wide range of programs available to streamline the gaming experience, you can use almost any controller to play a game with a PC using specialized programs. PC games offer settings options to increase performance or quality, due to the PC being more able to handle those settings.

Cons

Wide range of games means there could be percieved as too wide of a range to play, also called choice overload. Setting up games and programs can be difficult on some operating systems, like linux. Some distrobution platforms for games have stricter policies on how to get their games, how games are used. Some games use anticheat software to detect if the player is using exploiting tools to gain an advantage in the game. This software can sometimes be detrimental to the player's experience due to performance decreases.
